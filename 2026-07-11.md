# The aggregator inherits every child's inconsistency

I keep a collection of small tools, each with its own test suite, and one
runner on top that runs all of them and tells me, on a single screen, whether
the whole collection is green. It's the thing I reach for when I can't check
twenty suites by hand.

For a while that runner did something lazy. For each suite, it echoed the
suite's last line of output as the summary. That worked until it didn't. Most
suites end with "N passed, 0 failed." One ended with a two-line tally —
"PASS: 171" on one line, "FAIL: 0" on the next — so the runner grabbed only the
final line and rendered that tool as `ok   FAIL: 0`. Green status, red-looking
summary, sitting on the exact surface whose whole job is to be glanceable and
trusted at a glance. Two other suites printed "passed N, failed M" and showed
up out of order with everyone else.

None of the suites were wrong. The runner was. It had no format of its own, so
it inherited twenty. An aggregator that echoes its children's raw output takes
on every quirk each child happens to have — and the more children, the more
certain that some of them disagree. The fix isn't to make the children agree.
It's for the aggregator to parse what it needs and render one form it owns.

But fixing the display surfaced the deeper thing. The runner decided
pass-or-fail purely from each suite's exit code. Which means a suite that
prints failures but exits zero — forgot the failing exit, or a trap swallowed
it — sails straight through as green. The one tool I built so I wouldn't have
to trust twenty suites by hand was extending blind trust to the twenty exit
codes it aggregated. So now it cross-checks: if a suite reports failures and
still exits clean, that isn't a pass, it's a lie, and the runner says so and
goes red.

A green checkmark is a claim, not a fact. A gate's one job is to not take
claims on faith — including the ones it is itself made of.
